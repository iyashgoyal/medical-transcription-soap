# SOAP Note

## Subjective
- Patient reports pain on the outside of the right elbow starting several months ago, which has recently worsened.
- No known trauma or triggers for the pain; notes it feels worse at the end of the work day.
- Pain quality: achy, and sometimes sharp with certain movements.
- Aggravating factors: wrist movements (bending wrist down), picking things up, holding heavy objects at work (does repetitive line work).
- Relieving factors: resting the elbow helps; uses ibuprofen and over-the-counter topical ointments which take the edge off and help get through work.
- Pain severity: rated about a 4 out of 10.
- First time experiencing this type of pain; reports it is getting worse over the past several months.
- Right-handed; notes the pain is causing problems with daily activities and work.
- Pertinent negatives: no pain in other joints/muscles, no radiation of pain, no numbness or tingling, no cold sensation in the arm, no weight loss, no fever or chills, no fatigue, no rashes, no sensory changes, no cardiopulmonary or gastrointestinal symptoms, no urinary symptoms, no memory loss or confusion.
- Past medical history: healthy, no long-standing conditions.
- Past surgical history: C-section 17 years ago (non-emergency).
- Medications: Tylenol and Advil as needed for pain.
- Allergies: No known drug allergies.
- Social history: Non-smoker, no marijuana use, occasional alcohol use (glass of wine about once a week), no recent sick contacts or travel.

## Objective
- Inspection: Right elbow appears normal with no redness, swelling, misalignment, or asymmetry compared to the left elbow. No skin changes or deformities.
- Palpation: Tenderness present at the lateral aspect of the right elbow. No pain on palpation of the medial aspect, forearm, or biceps. Area is not warm to the touch.
- Range of Motion and Strength Testing:
  - Wrist extension with elbow in full extension: causes pain.
  - Wrist flexion with elbow in full extension: causes pain.
  - Elbow flexion: no pain.
  - Shoulder extension: no pain.
  - Shoulder adduction: normal, no pain.
  - Forearm pronation and supination: no pain.

## Assessment
- Lateral epicondylitis (Tennis elbow)

## Plan
- Activity modifications: avoid overly repetitive tasks at work, particularly those involving the forearm extensor muscles.
- Medications: continue OTC analgesics/NSAIDs (Tylenol, ibuprofen/Advil) for pain management as needed.
- Physical therapy: recommended to increase mobility and strength in the affected arm.
- Supportive care: use a compression sleeve and apply ice to the area for pain as needed.
- Imaging: none deemed necessary at this time as there was no trauma and low suspicion for fracture.
- Prognosis/Timeline: expected to resolve within a couple of months with physical therapy and conservative management, though total resolution can take up to six months to two years at most.
