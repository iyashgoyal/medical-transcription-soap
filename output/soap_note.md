# SOAP Note

## Subjective
- Chief complaint: Pain on the outside of the right elbow, starting several months ago and recently becoming more painful.
- Onset: Several months ago, getting worse over time; no trauma or triggers noted, felt more at the end of work.
- Aggravating factors: Bending the wrist down, picking things up, holding heavy objects at work, and repetitive line work.
- Relieving factors: Resting the elbow and taking ibuprofen or drugstore topical ointments.
- Pain quality: Achy, and sometimes sharp depending on movement.
- Severity: Rated as 4 out of 10.
- Radiation: Localized to one spot over the outside of the right elbow, not radiating upwards or downwards.
- Pertinent negatives: No previous experience of this type of pain, no aches or pains in other joint or muscle groups, no numbness or tingling, no cold feeling in the arm, no weight loss, no fever or chills, no fatigue, no rashes, no sensory changes (vision, hearing, smell, taste), no cough, shortness of breath, or wheezing, no chest pain, heart palpitations, or dizziness, no bowel movement changes, no nausea or vomiting, no urinary symptoms, and no memory loss or confusion.
- Past medical history: Otherwise healthy.
- Past surgical history: C-section for son approximately 17 years ago (non-emergency).
- Medications: Takes Tylenol and Advil (ibuprofen) for pain.
- Allergies: No allergies.
- Social history: Does not smoke, does not use marijuana, drinks a glass of wine about once a week, has no sick contacts, and no recent travel.
- Family history: No family history of skeletal, muscular, neurological, or autoimmune conditions.

## Objective
- General appearance: Healthy, right-handed.
- Inspection of right elbow: Looks the same as the other elbow, no redness, swelling, misalignment, asymmetry, skin changes, or deformities.
- Palpation: Tenderness to palpation at the lateral aspect of the right elbow; no pain at the medial aspect, forearm, or biceps. Area is not hot.
- Range of motion / Strength testing (guided self-examination):
  - Full range of motion of both the elbow and hand.
  - Pain with wrist extension with elbow in full extension.
  - Pain with wrist flexion with elbow in full extension.
  - No pain with elbow flexion.
  - No pain with shoulder extension or adduction.
  - No pain with forearm pronation and supination.

## Assessment
- Lateral epicondylitis (also known as tennis elbow), likely predisposed by repetitive strain at work and continuous contraction of the wrist extensor muscle group.

## Plan
- Imaging: Not deemed necessary as there was no trauma and no concern for fractures.
- Activity modifications: Avoid overly repetitive activities at work, especially those using extensor forearm muscles.
- Support: Use a compression sleeve.
- Medications: Continue taking Tylenol or NSAIDs (ibuprofen) for pain.
- Physical therapy: Recommended to increase mobility and strength in the arm.
- Modalities: Apply ice for pain and swelling.
- Prognosis: Should resolve in a couple of months with physical therapy, compression, and splints, lasting at most six months to two years.
